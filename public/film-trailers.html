The structure of a film trailer

60 min

Genre decides what it feels like. Structure decides what order it happens in — and nearly every trailer builds from the same set of beats.

Energy order, not story orderGrab, settle, build, explode, stop, land. That's the whole shape.
Logos movedThey used to open the trailer. Now they sit after the hook — nobody ever kept watching for a logo.
Silence sells the titleOne beat of nothing before the title card. Free, and the biggest upgrade you can make.

Task 5

Choose a trailer and build its timeline

35 min✓
  1. Pick a trailer — any genre, roughly 1:30–2:30 long, PG or M. Get it approved by your teacher, then watch it once with no notes.
  2. Watch it again with your finger on the pause key.
  3. Drag the cards by the handle so the list matches the order your trailer actually uses.
  4. Drag in the floating elements from the amber tray — tagline, critic quotes, accolades, actor names, release line, hashtag — and drop each one where it appears.
  5. Click a card to open it, then type the timecode.
  6. Tap the SWAT code buttons — symbolic, written, audio, technical — for whichever codes are doing the work in that beat. Tap as many as apply, tap again to turn one off.
  7. Write what happens and its effect on the audience. If a beat isn't used, write "not used" and what the editor did instead.

No two trailers run these beats in the same order, and that's the point — the order is a marketing decision. A horror trailer might hold the title until the last four seconds; a comedy might drop it halfway and spend the rest on jokes. Build the order you actually see.
